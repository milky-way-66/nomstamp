# Design review — 19 Aug 2026

A screenshot sweep of all 13 screens (light, dark, seeded, empty), captured with
`FoodMapUITests/DesignSweep.swift` on an iPhone 17 simulator. The seeded photographs are the test
fixture's gradient, so these notes judge layout and colour, never photography.

Findings are ordered by how much they cost the product, not by how hard they are to fix.

## 1. The map is Apple's, not ours

On first launch the only food on the map is **Apple's**: "Tony's Pizza", "Cafe Nagomi",
"Arsicault", in Apple's orange. On the seeded map, "Crab House at Pier 39" and "Hillstone
Restaurant" read louder than the user's own stamps. The app's whole premise — *your* places — is
undercut by the cartography.

The palette clashes too: cool grey streets and a large cyan bay meet a cream paper sheet with no
transition. Two different products touching each other.

**Direction:** exclude food POIs so the only food pins are the user's; warm the cartography with a
paper-tinted overlay; flat elevation; quieter controls.

## 2. The floating action cluster looks broken

On the empty map the "near me" button is a ghost — pale outline on pale cartography, reading as
disabled. The three glyphs are not one family (magnifier-with-arrow, bookmark, camera), the size
steps are arbitrary, and the cluster sits on top of Apple's attribution logo.

**Direction:** one family, one rhythm, solid paper fill with pandan glyphs, full contrast, clear
of the attribution.

## 3. The peek detent has a torn edge

At rest the sheet shows the search field, the filter, **and half of a white list row**. It reads as
a rendering glitch rather than "there is more below".

**Direction:** the peek ends cleanly after the search field; the filter appears on expansion.

## 4. The place detail loses its head

Back and ⋯ float on the photograph in translucent cream circles — low contrast against an image,
and they are the only chrome, so scrolling loses the place's name entirely. The hero is also
double-framed: a bordered card wrapped around an already-rounded photograph.

**Direction:** full-bleed cover — photograph behind the serif name with a paper scrim — plus a
compact sticky header on scroll.

## 5. Meal entries are identical blocks

Photograph, dish, `19 Aug 2026 at 16:02`, stars, repeat. No hierarchy between the dish and a
machine-formatted timestamp, no rhythm down the page, and "Add another meal" is a pale pink pill
that disappears.

**Direction:** editorial entries — numbered, letterspaced small-caps dates, notes in italic serif,
price where present — and a primary action with real weight.

## 6. The rating step is mostly empty space

The photograph floats in the upper middle of a large cream void; the stars are small thin
outlines; the two controls are unlabelled 16 pt arrows in the corners. It is the least designed
screen in the app and the one every meal passes through.

**Direction:** photograph to the top edge, 48 pt stars with a spring and a haptic, "Skip" and
"Next" as labelled buttons, a 2-of-3 step indicator.

## 7. Dark mode is generic

Near-black with salmon accents is legible and characterless: every trace of the paper identity is
gone. It could be any app.

**Direction:** *night market* — deep green ground, pandan and bay as neon, photographs glowing
rather than floating on black.

## 8. The empty state gets clipped

"Photograph a meal where you are, or save a place someone told you about." is cut off by the
sheet's peek, there is no button to act on, and the mark is a stock SF Symbol.

**Direction:** a taller peek for the empty case, two explicit actions, and a drawn mark.

## Sequencing

1. Map identity and the action cluster — fixes every screenshot in the app.
2. Place detail cover and the rating step — the two screens that carry the product.
3. Character: editorial entries, custom filter tabs, paper grain, night-market dark mode.
4. Delight: per-stamp tilt, spring selection, pin → cover transition, the passport grid.


## Progress, 19 Aug 2026 (branch `art-direction`)

- Stage 1 — printing primitives: paper grain, stamp tilt, misregistration, indigo ink.
- Stage 2 — drawn chrome (`FoodMark`), `InkTabs` in place of the segmented control, a peek that
  fades instead of slicing a row.
- Stage 3 — the palette moved from lacquer/jade on cream to **pandan/bay on cool paper** (the warm
  scheme read as dusty), the place detail opens on a full-bleed torn cover, and `RatingMood` makes
  the score visible everywhere: pin frame, row stamp, page rule, card edge.
- Stage 4 — the list is set as a printed **index** (numbered entries, perforated stamps, small-caps
  metadata), dark mode is the **night market** (grain that lights the page, neon inks, photographs
  that glow), and opening a place **zooms from its row's stamp into the cover**. Demo data now
  includes San Francisco, so the stamps are visible on a simulator's map.

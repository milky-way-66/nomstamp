# Design note, 21 August — the words, and how they sound

Two complaints, both about language: *some words feel uncomfortable*, and the app should read as
friendlier. As with the night-legibility note, the useful move was to stop arguing about taste and
read all 158 strings in one sitting, side by side with their Vietnamese. Doing that turns a matter
of feel into five faults, four of which are defects by rules the SRS already holds the app to.

The app's copy is not bad. That is what makes the problem hard to see: one part of it is the best
writing in the product, and the rest is not written at all.

---

## 1. The app has two voices, and only one of them was written

The friends flow is genuinely good. It explains, it warns, it gives the reader a reason:

> Both phones should show these four letters. If they don't, stop — someone else is answering.

> Eight is the most. Remove someone to make room — the cap is what keeps this a circle rather than
> a following.

Whoever wrote those was writing. Now the core loop — the screen every single meal passes through:

> Rating · Note · Dish name · Step 2 of 3 · Couldn't save

Those are field labels off a form, and `Step 2 of 3` is a progress counter talking about itself.
The result is that the app sounds warmest in the feature the fewest people will use, and blankest
in the one they use every day. **A friendlier app is not achieved by adding warm words. It is
achieved by giving the capture flow the voice the friends flow already has.**

## 2. The same thing has more than one name

Not a matter of tone — a straightforward defect. A reader learning the app has to learn each
synonym separately, and a translator is handed two entries where there is one idea.

| The one idea | The names it currently has | Keep |
|---|---|---|
| Start logging a meal | `Add a meal`, `Add meal`, `Add another meal` | `Add a meal` / `Add another meal` |
| Waiting on a fix | `Finding where you are…`, `Finding you…` | `Finding where you are…` |
| Places near the reader | `Near me`, `Nearby`, `Saved places near me` | `Near me` |
| A place the reader ate at | `Been here`, `I ate here` | both, but `I ate here` only as an action |
| Score a meal | `Rate this meal`, `How was it?`, `Rating` | `How was it?` asks; `Rating` labels |

`Add a meal` and `Add meal` are the worst of these: two catalogue keys, two Vietnamese
translations, one button in the reader's mind.

## 3. Errors say what broke and then stop

**NFR-4.4** — *error messages shall state what happened and what the user can do*. Three do not:

| String | What it fails to say |
|---|---|
| `Couldn't save` | Whether the photo is lost, and whether to try again |
| `Can't add them` | Why, or what to do about it |
| `Can't tell where you are` | That the meal can still be saved without it |

There is a fourth error that has no words at all: if `fileDataRepresentation()` returns nil the
camera silently drops the shot (`InAppCamera.swift:99`). The reader taps the shutter and nothing
happens. That one is ADR-011's problem, not this note's, but it is the same failure of manners.

Two empty states have the same shape — a dead end where the reader is standing:
`Nothing saved near here`, `Nothing found nearby.` Neither offers the action that would fix it.

## 4. The Vietnamese is complete, and in places it is not Vietnamese

All 158 keys are translated — TC-N-01 proves the app *switches* language. Nothing proves the
result reads like something a person would say. Two are outright defects:

- **`No radio on this phone` → `Điện thoại này không có sóng`.** In everyday Vietnamese *sóng* is
  the mobile signal. Standing alone as a property of the phone, this tells the reader their phone
  has **no reception**, which is both alarming and untrue — the app means it has no Bluetooth. →
  `Máy này không có Bluetooth`.

  The word is not banned. Two other strings use it well — `qua sóng` (*over the radio*) and
  `ra ngoài tầm sóng` (*out of radio range*) — where a modifier makes the sense plain. Only the
  bare claim about the phone is wrong.
- **`Stamped by` → `Được đóng tem bởi`.** The `được … bởi` passive is a calque from English and
  reads as machine translation. Vietnamese says who did it: `Tem của Lan`, or `Lan đã đóng tem`.
  It is in the two heading strings a friend-stamped place shows — `Stamped by` and
  `Also stamped by` — so it sits at the top of the section rather than buried in it.

  The six VoiceOver strings underneath escaped the passive but introduced a second fault: they say
  `đã đóng dấu` — *stamped a seal* — where the rest of the app says `tem`. A Vietnamese
  screen-reader user therefore hears a different noun for the product's central object than the
  one on screen. Both go to `tem`.


And three that are merely uncomfortable, which is what was asked about:

- **`Rate this meal` → `Chấm điểm bữa này`.** *Chấm điểm* is what a teacher does to homework. It
  puts the reader in the position of marking their dinner. The catalogue already uses the natural
  word — `Đánh giá` — for the noun, so the harsher verb is also the inconsistent one.
- **`Remove` → `Xoá`, and `Remove and delete their stamps` → `Xoá bạn và xoá luôn tem của họ`.**
  *Xoá bạn* is "delete the friend" — said about a person, and said twice in one sentence. Removing
  a connection is `Bỏ kết nối`; the stamps are what gets `xoá`.
- **`Camera access is off` → `Máy ảnh đang bị tắt quyền`.** Grammatically strained, and passive
  about who did it. `Nomstamp chưa được dùng máy ảnh` names the app, not the reader's mistake.

## 5. A few words put the reader in the wrong

Small, and the reason the app "feels uncomfortable" even where nothing is wrong:

| Now | The problem | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| `You can only add someone you are sitting with.` | A restriction imposed on the reader | `Everyone here is someone you sat down with.` |
| `Eight friends is the most. Remove someone first.` | An order | `Eight is the most. Make room by removing someone.` |
| `Can't tell where you are` | The app failing, phrased as the reader being lost | `Still finding you` |
| `They need this screen open too.` | Fine, but reads as blame when it appears under a failure | Keep, moved above the failure |
| `Use my exact spot` | *spot* is doing work *place* should do | `Use where I am` |

## The five rules

Everything above collapses to five rules, which is what the tests will enforce and what any new
string is written against.

1. **Say the next thing to do.** Every error and every empty state ends with something the reader
   can act on — a sentence or a button, not a full stop.
2. **One idea, one word.** The lexicon below is the whole vocabulary. A new synonym is a bug.
3. **The app is at fault, never the reader.** Name what the app has not done yet; never phrase a
   permission or a limit as the reader's failing.
4. **Vietnamese is written, not translated.** No `được … bởi` passive; no English sentence with
   Vietnamese words in it. Where the two languages want different sentences, they get different
   sentences.
5. **The capture flow gets the friends flow's voice.** The screen every meal passes through is the
   one worth writing.

## The lexicon

| Idea | English | Vietnamese |
|---|---|---|
| Log a meal | meal | bữa |
| A saved location | place | địa điểm |
| Ate there | been here | đã đến |
| Not yet been | want to try | muốn thử |
| A friend's shared place | stamp | tem *(never* dấu*)* |
| Score a meal | rating (noun) / how was it? (question) | đánh giá |
| Sever a friendship | remove | bỏ kết nối |
| Destroy data | delete | xoá |
| Bluetooth | Bluetooth | Bluetooth *(never* sóng*)* |

`remove` and `delete` are deliberately split: removing a friend is about a relationship and is
re-doable by sitting down together again; deleting is about data and is not. The app has been
using one word for both, in both languages.

## What is now guaranteed

Copy has never had a test in this project; the catalogue was checked for *completeness* (TC-N-01)
and never for *content*. Four cheap assertions, all reading the catalogue as data:

- Every catalogue key has a Vietnamese unit, and no two English values are identical — the
  duplicate-synonym class of bug (TC-N-30).
- No Vietnamese value uses the `được … bởi` passive, states a phone's radio as a bare `sóng`, or
  calls a stamp a `dấu` (TC-N-31).
- Every string in the error and empty-state set names an action (TC-N-32).
- The lexicon holds: no shipped string uses a retired synonym (TC-N-33).

The last three are lint, not judgement. No test can tell whether a sentence is friendly. What they
can do is stop the four faults above from coming back one string at a time, which is exactly how
they arrived.

# Second pass, same day — the other 123 strings

The first pass rewrote the strings that were obviously wrong. Reading the remainder — the ones that
looked fine — found six more faults, one of which is a defect against a test case the project
already has and which currently passes.

## 6. Three of the eight friend inks are the same word in Vietnamese

**TC-N-27** exists so that *whose a stamp is* can always be **said**, not only shown — it is the
accessibility backstop under ADR-010's decision to stop drawing provenance on the map. It asserts
the eight ink words are distinct. It passes. It checks the English.

In Vietnamese the plate reads:

| Ink | English | Vietnamese |
|---|---|---|
| harbour | harbour | **xanh** biển |
| leaf | leaf | **xanh** lá |
| teal | teal | **xanh** mòng két |

Three of eight open with the same word. A Vietnamese VoiceOver user hearing *"mực xanh…"* has to
wait out the whole phrase to learn which of three friends stamped a place — and the filter, which
FR-12.9 makes *how the map is read* rather than a convenience, is where they hear it most.

The English plate is fine and does not change. The Vietnamese becomes:

| Ink | Was | Becomes | First word |
|---|---|---|---|
| harbour | xanh biển | `xanh biển` | xanh |
| leaf | xanh lá | `lá non` | lá |
| teal | xanh mòng két | `mòng két` | mòng |

*mòng két* is the teal duck the colour is named after in both languages, so nothing is invented —
the Vietnamese was simply carrying a redundant `xanh` in front of two names that did not need it.

**TC-N-27 is extended to run over every localisation, not the English enum.** A test that proves a
property of the app in one language and is silent in the other is worth less than it appears.

## 7. Two keys print the same sentence

`· %@ meals` and `· %lld meals` are both in the catalogue. The second is correct — a count is an
integer, and it has the plural variations Vietnamese and English need. The first takes a
**string**, which means somewhere a number is being formatted before it reaches the catalogue, and
that path cannot pluralise at all. One of the two is a latent bug wearing the other's clothes.
`· %@ meals` goes; its call site moves to the counted key.

Same class, less dangerous: `Already on your map`, `They are already on your map.` and
`Can't add them` are three strings for one situation. The first pass caught the third. All three
now resolve to **They're already here** / `Họ đã ở đây rồi`.

## 8. The app has two vocabularies for on and off

| Concept | Words in use |
|---|---|
| A layer is visible | `On`, `Off`, `Show`, `Showing`, `Hidden` |
| Vietnamese | `Bật`, `Tắt`, `Hiển thị`, `Đang hiện`, `Đang ẩn` |

Five words, two of them states and three of them actions, used interchangeably across the friends
filter and the layer control. The rule: **`Show` / `Hide` are what you do; `Showing` / `Hidden` are
what is true.** `On` and `Off` retire — they describe a switch, and the reader is not thinking
about a switch, they are thinking about whether they can see Lan's places.

Vietnamese follows exactly: `Hiện` / `Ẩn` for the actions, `Đang hiện` / `Đang ẩn` for the states.
`Bật` and `Tắt` retire with their English.

## 9. `điểm` and `đánh giá` are both the rating

The first pass moved `Chấm điểm bữa này` to `Món này thế nào?` and noted `đánh giá` as the lexicon
word. It missed that the noun is inconsistent everywhere else:

| Key | Was | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| `Average rating` | Điểm trung bình | Đánh giá trung bình |
| `Their rating` | Điểm của họ | Đánh giá của họ |
| `Skip the rating` | Bỏ qua **chấm điểm** | Bỏ qua đánh giá |
| `Rating` | Đánh giá | *(unchanged — it was always right)* |

`Bỏ qua chấm điểm` is the one that matters: the first pass removed the schoolteacher's verb from
the visible button and left it on the VoiceOver label beside it.

## 10. `máy` is doing three jobs

`Máy này không có máy ảnh` — *this machine has no camera-machine*. `Máy` is a phone here, a camera
there, and in `Đang tìm các máy trong phòng…` it is other people's phones. Vietnamese has the word:
**`điện thoại`**.

This also corrects the first pass, which proposed `Máy này không có Bluetooth` and inherited the
problem:

| Key | First pass said | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `No camera here` | Máy này không có máy ảnh | **Điện thoại này không có máy ảnh** |
| `No radio on this phone` | Máy này không có Bluetooth | **Điện thoại này không có Bluetooth** |
| `Looking for phones in the room…` | *(untouched)* | **Đang tìm điện thoại trong phòng…** |

`Máy` survives only where it means a camera body, which is nowhere in this app.

## 11. Smaller things

| Key | Was | Becomes | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Times they went` | Times they went / Số lần họ đến | **Visits** / Số lần đến | The English was never a phrase anyone says |
| `Matching word` | Từ đối chiếu | **Từ xác nhận** | *Đối chiếu* is what an auditor does to a ledger |
| `Remove this photo` | Bỏ ảnh này | **Xoá ảnh này** | A photo is data, and the lexicon gives data `xoá` |
| `Tags, comma separated` | Thẻ, cách nhau bằng dấu phẩy | **`bún chả, Hà Nội`** | A placeholder should show the shape, not describe it |
| `OK` | OK | *(named for its action)* | An alert button says what it does |
| ` of 5` | ` of 5` | *(folded into the counted string)* | A key that is a fragment with a leading space cannot be translated safely |
| `Use a test photo` | *(shipped)* | *(leaves the catalogue)* | A UI-testing affordance, translated into Vietnamese, shipped to readers |

`Use a test photo` is the only one that is a mistake rather than a judgement. It is gated behind
`AppDependencies.isUITesting` so no reader sees the button — but the string is in the shipped
catalogue and someone translated it. Test scaffolding takes a literal, not a catalogue key.

## What the second pass changes about the rules

Rule 4 said *Vietnamese is written, not translated*. Findings 6, 9 and 10 sharpen it into something
testable:

> **4a.** A property the app guarantees in English — distinct ink words, one word per idea — is
> guaranteed in **every** language, or it is not guaranteed. Tests that read the catalogue read all
> of it.

That is the whole lesson of TC-N-27 passing while three friends share a word.
